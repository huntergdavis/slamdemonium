### What each class contributes

The contemporary [player vehicle guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps2/919649-burnout-3-takedown/faqs/33851) lists separate **normal speed, boost speed and weight ratings per model**. Its weight explanation says heavy cars accelerate more slowly, feel more rigid, carry more impact momentum and resist being pushed. These are coarse game ratings, not kilograms or engine litres. Handling descriptions below are the player-feel implication of that guide and the [contemporary review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/burnout-3-takedown-review/1900-6106835/); individual models vary.

| B3 garage slot      | What the driver gets                                                                    | Weight / speed / boost distinction                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Compact             | Starting, readable small cars; easier time to recognize and correct a mistake.          | Mostly weight 1–2; the lower race-series speed band. Boost speed still exceeds most normal-speed ceilings.                                                            |
| Muscle              | A harder shunt and more settled, less flickable turning in its heavier examples.        | Weight 2–5: Type 1 is a heavy 5, other Muscle variants are lighter. Faster upper limits than Compact; the heavy Type 1 has a much larger normal-to-boost speed gap.   |
| Coupe               | Middle step for players wanting a fast, lower body without the lightest Sports feel.    | Weight 2–3; speed ceilings rise over the starter series. Different Coupe variants trade ordinary and boosted top speed.                                               |
| Sports              | Fast, light and responsive, with less momentum in a side-to-side fight.                 | Usually weight 1, with a heavier exception; boosted ceiling is above Coupe. More speed makes traffic gaps shorter in time.                                            |
| Super               | The fastest main race class: route knowledge and early steering matter more.            | Weight 2–3, so fast does not mean light. Normal and boosted ceilings are the highest main-series band, though some variants have only a small top-speed gap.          |
| Special             | Trophy cars with memorable shapes/voices, such as hotrod and circuit racers.            | Weight and speed vary; no single “Special handling” law. They are rewards, not the next compulsory power tier.                                                        |
| Pickup / SUV        | Big, more resistant crash choices with a different sight line and collision silhouette. | Heavy Pickup and SUV Deluxe carry weight 5 in the guide; both are in B3's Crash-only Heavyweight series.                                                              |
| Van / truck         | Traffic-pileup tools: they can push and block more than a light race car.               | Van weight 3, delivery truck 5, tractor/longnose cabs 7/9; Crash-only as selectable cars. Their large mass is a scoring/impact choice, not a normal race boost bonus. |
| Bus / service heavy | A slow-turning, huge visual and collision target that anchors a pileup.                 | City bus and fire truck weight 10; trash truck 8. Crash-only selectable cars.                                                                                         |

**Boost is not a class stat in this table.** The guide's “Top Boost Speed” differs from normal top speed by model, while [the manual's boost-earning rules](https://oldgamesdownload.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/burnout-3-takedown_xbox_manual_en_7mq.pdf) apply to dangerous moves and takedowns. A heavy car may make a shunt easier; it does not have a documented extra class-specific boost payout. [Vehicle inventory](https://burnout.fandom.com/wiki/Vehicles_%28Burnout_3%29)

**How to interpret “engine size.”** Burnout 3's player-facing garage gives series, speed/weight and unlocked variants, not a licensed engine-displacement simulator. For Slamdemonium, use an engine identity (idle/redline, firing cadence, exhaust timbre, low-speed pull, boost sound) and a measurable car feel. A “small engine compact” versus “large engine muscle” label should earn its place through acceleration and sound A/B drives. Keep the CTO's long-gear, redline-cruise, boost-only top-gear decisions; never infer physical gearing from the current virtual RPM display. This is a design inference from the garage and our [RPM contract](../DECISIONS.md#derived-rpm-and-virtual-gears-are-presentation-state-2026-09-21), not a claim about Criterion's engine implementation.

## 2. The roadmap: ordered, drive-approved vertical slices

### Measurement and approval contract for every slice

**The CTO drives the candidate browser build before approving a slice.** A green test or a demo video is supporting evidence, not the verdict. Every candidate PR reports a same-day `main` run and candidate run on the **same i5-8250U / Intel UHD 620**, headed Chromium, 1280×720, the fixed 45-second circuit route in [`browser-drive.mjs`](../../scripts/perf/browser-drive.mjs), with the GL renderer string (Vulkan hardware path versus a software fallback) in the report. One browser runs at a time. Report fps, full-step p50/p99, Jolt p50/p99, route distance, and in-frame traffic counts at **4 px and 8 px**; name the camera projection and denominator. No headless or Node number can pass this browser gate. The CTO's own machine is an additional absolute drive gate, including responsiveness and perceived speed. [Browser protocol and historical main `e6ea7da` result](../PERFORMANCE.md#the-browser-gate-2026-10-01)

The historical comparison point is **12.2 fps** and **5.7 ms full-step p99** at 720p on that box, with 35 cars in frame median on #175; these are context, **not** a permanent baseline to game. The latest `main` is the denominator for every PR. Let **F** be median fps and **P** full-step p99 over the fixed drive. Unless a slice gives a stricter number, require `F_candidate ≥ 0.95 × F_main`, `P_candidate ≤ 1.10 × P_main`, and no shorter route distance or slower simulation advance. After slice **R1**, the added target is **at least 20 fps at 720p**; after **R2**, **30 fps**. These floors are deliberate stop/go targets, not predictions. During an authored crash burst, also publish a separate two-second window: awake bodies, burst p99 and lowest fps. A transient pileup is judged in its own scene, never averaged away in the 45-second drive. No threshold is “fixed” by changing resolution, traffic density, camera, renderer, or the route between A/B runs.

**Measure the game the player sees.** Where a slice names 20, 30, 35 or more visible cars, count projected car bounding boxes at 4 px and 8 px, not records in a spawn array or cars within a world radius. Landing load is reported as **closing speed in m/s**, not the clamped `impactSeverity` scale. The game's 120 Hz physics remains a fixed step; the original 2 ms manual-step CI budget and the newer 5.7 ms real-browser dense-traffic p99 are different workloads and must be shown separately. [Jump convention](../../BACKLOG.md#found-while-proving-the-jump-2026-09-29) [performance modes](../PERFORMANCE.md#two-modes-one-drive)

### Two technical choices that govern the plan

**Car fidelity: use a coherent CC0 low-poly glTF kit as a starting shape library, then art-direct and optimize it.** “Higher definition” here means cars recognizable at chase distance and in a crash: distinct compact/coupe/muscle/sports/SUV/truck/bus proportions, wheelbase, windows, lamps, grille, paint/roughness breakup, readable nose/rear, and a damaged state. Raw polygon count is a poor proxy. The existing box parts are cheap and an honest placeholder, but they read as boxes. Hand-authoring all 20+ silhouettes consumes the art schedule. Fully procedural body generation produces variety cheaply but needs a large tuning and quality pass to avoid generic, malformed cars. A raw licensed/CC0 marketplace glTF collection can give attractive isolated models but inconsistent scale, axis, material count, wheels, silhouette and legal provenance. Start with [Kenney's CC0 Car Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/car-kit), which has 45 files; [prior car research](next-features.md#3-cars-choose-one-coherent-kit-and-normalize-it-once) already recommends a versioned shared vehicle definition. Import once, normalize metres / +Y up / -Z forward, flatten repeated static parts, keep wheel and break-off pivots, and use a small shared material palette. Preserve source and license with assets. These are planning budgets to A/B on the UHD 620: **hero car ≤12k triangles and ≤8 main-pass draws**, near traffic **≤2k triangles and ≤2 draws each**, far traffic **≤200 triangles and one instanced draw per silhouette/material bucket**; at most one 512² shared atlas for the first set and no per-car 1024² textures. Derive LOD from projected size (8 px and 4 px), not a blind distance alone. [Three.js glTF](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html), [LOD](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LOD.html), [instancing](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html), [object-count costs](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/optimize-lots-of-objects.html). The old 15k-triangle/10-draw first-car proposal predates the measured 12.2 fps dense-traffic render bottleneck; treat it as an upper research estimate, not an automatic allowance.

| Car-art route                       | What it buys / costs on this browser target                                                                                                   | Decision                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Existing box parts                  | Fastest iteration, shared primitive geometry; no credible close-up body, windows or damage silhouette.                                        | Keep as physics/debug fallback.                                                    |
| Hand-built stylised low-poly meshes | Coherent look, exact pivots and deformation zones; every additional body style consumes art time.                                             | Use for a hero correction or missing heavy vehicle, not the entire initial roster. |
| CC0 or licensed glTF packs          | Many recognizable shapes quickly; unknown node/material/texture counts, mixed coordinate systems and per-file license obligations.            | Normalize one coherent CC0 kit; do not ship raw pack files directly.               |
| Procedural body generation          | Cheap combinatorial variety and consistent parameters; quality, recognizable class identity and crash topology need substantial authoring.    | Later variant tool after the art target is established.                            |
| LOD + instancing                    | Near-car detail where pixels support it, low draw count for far traffic; requires stable material buckets and careful handoff to Jolt bodies. | Required for every selected route, measured at 8 px/4 px.                          |

**Crash fidelity: deform the visible traffic car first, then change its rigid collision shape to fit.** Criterion described rewritten deformation systems, twisting/torn cars and opening panels in Burnout 3; the [developer interview](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/burnout-3-qanda/1100-6089105/) and [game manual](https://oldgamesdownload.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/burnout-3-takedown_xbox_manual_en_7mq.pdf) do **not** establish whether or how Burnout 3 changed collision geometry. Matching the crushed collider is the CTO's Slamdemonium requirement, not a verified claim about Criterion's implementation. P0A changes traffic crash response after spacing #178; P0B adds persistent visible nose/rear/side crush after A0; the new **P0C immediately follows P0B** and swaps a prebuilt crushed convex shape onto each affected traffic body. The [pinned WASM binding](https://unpkg.com/jolt-physics@1.1.0/dist/types.d.ts) exposes `BodyInterface.SetShape`, matching [Jolt's documented API](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysicsDocs/5.4.0/class_body_interface.html). Jolt's current WASM binding reports contact pairs but **no solved pair impulse**; classify isolated hits by contact normal and relative/closing speed in m/s, with cooldown and known limits for overlapping contacts. [Jolt research](jolt-integration.md#r1b-solved-contact-impulse-is-not-exposed-by-jolt-110) P2 later adds bounded detachable pieces and pooled debris. True soft-body cars, per-contact convex cooking, per-vertex collision meshes and a physics-engine swap are out. **Player-car visible damage remains an open CTO decision** in P3, outside P0A/P0B/P0C/P2.

The useful arcade illusion is **real rigid-body contact with a collision shape that follows staged crush**, plus a directional dent, a small number of detached pieces, a short energy-absorbing response, sound/particles and a camera angle that lets the player read the wreck. This is a Slamdemonium design choice informed by [Criterion's stated presentation goal](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/burnout-3-qanda/1100-6089105/), not a claim that Criterion used Jolt, discrete convex swaps or any known collider-deformation method. Any hit involving several simultaneous bodies needs explicit “estimated/ambiguous” damage attribution; do not turn Jolt's unavailable solved impulse into an invented exact number.

### Slice order and drive gates

**W0 — Traffic spacing and rule-bound density (world/traffic; already approved and in flight).** After this, the player can read pockets, gaps and lane rules across the whole circuit instead of an evenly packed procession.

- **Scope:** In: the Codex spacing ruleset already assigned in NS4, with deterministic seed and whole-lap traffic. Out: new car meshes, rival AI, traffic checking and boost-rate retuning.
- **Drive approval:** Run the circuit twice, pass and miss traffic at 25–60 m/s, and point to repeatable sparse and busy sections without a sudden empty horizon.
- **Gate:** In-frame 4 px median **≥20** and 8 px median **≥12**, no continuous open straight over **5 s** with zero 8 px cars on the fixed 45 s route; `F/P` base rule and actual oncoming/near-miss grants at **>15 m/s** only. Publish 4/8 px p10/median/p90 and seed. The 20-car floor preserves the CTO's earlier explicit request while allowing less uniform spacing. [BACKLOG NS4](../../BACKLOG.md#next-slice-as-set-by-the-cto-2026-09-24)
- **Dependencies / effort:** Existing #175 traffic LOD. **M**, gameplay/UX + physics. Can run alongside A0's asset preparation with the agreed single `modelKind` field handoff; one integrator owns the shared traffic file.
- **Risk / cheapest probe:** Randomness accidentally creates empty boost stretches. Before art, run ten seeds through the fixed projection counter and drive the worst p10 seed.

**A0 — Larger, varied traffic silhouettes (cars/assets; already approved and in flight).** After this, the player can distinguish a small car from a coupe, SUV or work vehicle at speed.

- **Scope:** In: the assigned six-kind catalogue, 30–40% bigger screen presence and `modelKind` handoff. Out: a playable garage, a raw marketplace import, per-model engine physics or new traffic AI.
- **Drive approval:** Pass each of the six kinds at 25–45 m/s on the circuit; identify them by outline at chase distance, then hit two and confirm the collider never looks detached from its shape.
- **Gate:** **Six** distinct shapes, size increase **30–40%** versus pre-increment screenshot at fixed camera, 4/8 px counts reported, no visual/physics width mismatch above **0.3 m** at impact; `F/P` base rule. [BACKLOG NS4](../../BACKLOG.md#next-slice-as-set-by-the-cto-2026-09-24)
- **Dependencies / effort:** #175 and W0's one-field contract. **M**, rendering/assets. Asset authoring can run with W0; integration waits for its `traffic.ts` change.
- **Risk / cheapest probe:** Larger projected area raises fill cost. Render six unlit silhouettes at the target count on the UHD 620 before polishing materials.

**A0P — Player car reaches traffic scale (cars/assets + handling; CTO approved).** After this, the existing player car reads as the same physical scale as the enlarged traffic fleet without losing its familiar speed.

- **Scope:** In: grow the current player car's visible length and width **30–40%** against a fixed-camera pre-change capture; move wheelbase/track and resize its rigid collider to match the body; tune only what the new footprint needs to keep current acceleration, steering and speed ceilings. Out: a new authored model, class roster, player damage, a camera zoom that fakes size and traffic-size changes beyond A0.
- **Drive approval:** Follow and pass A0 compact/SUV traffic at **30/60/85 m/s**, then drift, brake, boost and strike a traffic car; the player body and wheels should read at the intended scale and never visibly clear a car or barrier its collider hits.
- **Gate:** Visual body length and width **1.30–1.40×** the prior player at the same camera; four measured visual-to-collider edge samples within **0.3 m**; nonboost and boost speed ceilings each within **±2 m/s** of the same-day approved pre-scale profile (the A2 class tolerance), with **0–30 m/s time within 5%**; `F/P` base rule. Record the measured profile before scaling so the comparison is reproducible.
- **Dependencies / effort:** A0 scale reference and W0/P0A's shared player/traffic contact seam stable. **S**, player vehicle visuals + physics tuning; one integrator owns player dimensions while A0 owns traffic assets. A1 later replaces the placeholder body at the same approved physical scale.
- **Risk / cheapest probe:** A larger footprint may clip lanes or dull steering. Scale only the existing player body/collider first, drive the narrowest circuit passage and the 30/60/85 m/s route, then tune before authoring new art.

**P0A — Traffic impact stops bouncing (crash/physics; approved as CRUMPLE A).** After this, the player can ram a traffic car and watch it tumble and settle instead of springing away.

- **Scope:** In: closing speed from contact normal and relative velocity, zero restitution on the struck traffic car, bounded normal separation with tangential motion free, and raised angular damping. Within each window, work may proceed in parallel **only** on distinct modules. `main.ts`, shared contact attribution, `traffic.ts`, map selection and persistent event results each have one temporary integrator; a second developer sends an interface handoff rather than editing the same file. A0/W0's agreed field is the sole planned shared-file exception. P0B's zone/stage state lands before P0C's collision work, and A0P's physical scale lands before A1's authored car. The PM can approve any slice by its bold name; the next slice starts only after its dependency's drive verdict, even when asset preparation runs ahead.

**Completion is a player experience, not a Burnout 3 inventory clone.** The minimum Burnout-class browser target here is three legible road identities, six routes, seven event verbs, nine meaningfully different selectable silhouettes, a player car at traffic scale, dense traffic that feeds boost, a rival takedown with camera payoff, one excellent Crash junction, visible directional traffic wreck damage with a matching crushed collision shape, instant retry and persistent progression at the hardware gate. P3 would add player-car damage if the CTO approves that open question. Burnout 3's larger 173-event tour, multiplayer and soundtrack are evidence of breadth, not prerequisites to this first complete browser experience.
