# Authored hero car (A1) — build plan

**Player outcome.** Choose one recognizable, unbranded car in at least three colors. Its wheels steer, spin and follow suspension; headlamps and brake lamps read from the chase camera; its engine voice responds to idle, throttle, shifting and boost. A hard hit still leaves visible player damage. The car remains the current 4.8 × 2.16 m playable vehicle, with the same collision, handling and recovery. This is [roadmap A1](https://github.com/huntergdavis/slamdemonium/pull/179) after A0P and R1.

Normalize only the chosen car once to metres, **+Y up / -Z forward**, with named FL/FR/RL/RR wheel pivots. Keep one shared 512² palette, reuse material instances for three paint colors and author a genuinely simplified far LOD; switching LOD must not move the car or hide its lamps. Initial targets: **≤300 KB added compressed transfer**, **≤12k hero triangles**, **≤8 main-pass draws**, and **≤1.34 MiB decoded RGBA8 palette including mipmaps**. The Kenney files suggest transfer headroom; actual network bytes, GPU texture use, triangles and draw calls must be logged in the built browser. A 1024² map alone needs about 5.33 MiB decoded with mipmaps, so additional normal/roughness maps and clearcoat wait for measured headroom. **Scope out:** a car roster, branded models, new drivetrain or collider shape, traffic model changes, expensive dynamic headlamp shadows, sampled multi-RPM engine bank and new damage physics. A1 owns the hero renderer, asset and engine profile; any concurrent camera/audio or map work needs a single `main.ts` integration owner.

## Acceptance and risks

The CTO chooses all three colors, completes a circuit lap and tight chicane, takes the big ramp and a loop, makes a hard player-car hit and recovers. Wheels stay planted at normal ride height, steer/spin in the right direction and follow suspension through the jump. Headlamps identify the nose; brake lamps respond to actual braking. In chase and impact views, the damaged shell remains attached and the four sampled visible-body points stay **≤0.3 m** from the current collider. No imported geometry blocks the camera. With no physics change, speed ceilings at 15/35/60 m/s stay within **±2 m/s** of same-day main and 0–30 m/s time within **5%**; compare equivalent input traces rather than an easier route.

On the i5-8250U / Intel UHD 620, run headed 1280×720 Chromium with vsync off, **main / PR / main / PR**, one browser on the GPU. Same route, seed, resolution and in-frame traffic: median fps **≥0.95× main**, full-step p99 **≤1.10× main**, no slower simulation advance, and the R1 **≥20 fps** floor. Record renderer, transferred asset bytes, texture allocation, hero triangles and main/shadow-pass draws separately; the CTO's M1 drive decides visual/audio approval. [Browser method](../PERFORMANCE.md#the-browser-gate-2026-10-01)

**First risks to retire:** a named wheel node may have an unusable axle or scale; verify import pivots against the current 0.44 m radius before art work. The imported body may resist today's per-vertex crush; deform an isolated hood/rear/side test mesh in PR 1 before replacing the whole visual. Extra shadow draws or material variants may cost more than raw triangles; measure actual renderer counters on the first imported build. The worklet profile may mask crash audio; make a matched-speed quiet/crash A/B with the existing master mute and limiter. If an asset fails a gate, keep the current playable visual while fixing that specific defect.
