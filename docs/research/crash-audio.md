# Crash audio: make the impact feel like metal

**Status:** Research for CTO approval; no sound files or runtime changes in this PR. Candidate licences and published durations were checked on 9 October 2026. Acoustic suitability still needs an in-game audition.

## What Burnout actually did

Criterion sound designer Ben Minto describes the first two *Burnout* games as a physics-driven collage: bonnet on tarmac, headlight and windscreen breaks, wheel-arch scrape, chassis bend. The events matched the picture but could sound thin. When licensed music arrived from *Burnout 3* onward, the team reused its four-channel music player for two stereo crash streams. A loud initial impact led into roughly 30 seconds of mangling, creaks and strain. The normal stream followed crash intensity and filled gaps between individual one-shots; a separate, surreal slow-motion stream began with the same bang. They rotated banks of metal and glass variants to avoid hearing the same crash repeatedly. This is Minto's account of the **series' evolving system**, not proof that every detail shipped unchanged in every *Burnout 3* platform build. [Minto interview](https://designingsound.org/2017/08/31/burnout-a-sound-design-retrospective-with-ben-minto/)

The useful principle is a **short attack plus an evolving body/tail**, with component sounds added only when the event warrants them. A single dull hit cannot communicate bent steel, glass, road drag and falling trim. The slow-motion *pitch drop* below is our proposed browser treatment; Minto specifically describes an alternate slow-motion stream, not a documented global pitch-shift algorithm. Preserve the game's procedural engine through the event, but duck it briefly so the crash reads. Minto also describes engines, road, transmission and turbo as separate changing layers, rather than letting one constant engine tone dominate. [Minto interview](https://designingsound.org/2017/08/31/burnout-a-sound-design-retrospective-with-ben-minto/)

## Current game seam

[`estimateImpactSeverity`](../../src/core/impactSeverity.ts) already gives normal **closing speed**, energy and a canonical 0–1 severity: zero at 0.8 m/s, full at 18.8 m/s. A scrape is tangential travel at contact, not a high normal closing speed. Stock Jolt generally provides no solved impulse here, so this severity is usually an estimate from pre-step relative velocity. [`AudioDirector`](../../src/audio/director.ts) currently queues a scalar impact, enforces a 120 ms per-body cooldown, and selects one surface-profile sample. The [`Howler` output](../../src/audio/howlerOutput.ts) has five continuous voices and ten transient slots under a fifteen-voice ceiling. This explains the present short “pud”: the system has no car-body crunch, glass, debris or sustained metal-grind event. The existing [`audio README`](../../src/audio/README.md) documents activation, mute and slow-motion handling; new layers should use that path.

## Runtime recipe

The thresholds below use **normal closing speed at contact**, not dashboard speed. They are initial mix breakpoints for listening tests, not new physics rules. Use canonical severity for loudness and layer selection. A contact's sideways speed and duration control grind independently. Start each hit with one dry transient, then add a slower metal-crush/body tail where called for; glass is conditional, never a universal crash garnish.

| Event | Normal closing speed and trigger | Audible layers and priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light knock | 0.8–3.5 m/s, first real contact | One short, bright metal tick/body tap; tiny creak only if crush grew. It must register even when the visual dent is small. Below 0.8 m/s, allow a quiet contact tick or scrape, not a “hard hit” sample. |
| Medium hit | 3.5–9 m/s | Dry attack + short panel bend/can crush + 0.15–0.5 s body rattle. Glass only if the struck zone plausibly includes a window and the hit is strong enough. |
| Hard slam | 9–18.8 m/s; 18.8+ stays at full severity | Hard attack + broad crunch/body tail + short low thump. Add glass for a window hit and a few debris ticks at the tail, with a short creak after the first bang. Cap gain rather than making 40 m/s twice as loud as 20 m/s. |
| Wall shunt | Same normal speed tiers, wall/roadside contact | Use a drier metal/concrete attack and panel crush; add scrape if sliding along the wall. A pole/prop may ring briefly. Avoid a glass burst for every wall touch. |
| Scrape / grind | Sustained contact for ≥80 ms, sideways slip ≥2 m/s, meaningful contact load | Start a rasp loop with a 30–50 ms attack; pitch/brightness and gain follow slip speed/load; crossfade 30–50 ms across loop seam; release within 100–180 ms after contact ends. One loop per contact pair, at most two audible near loops. Keep the initial knock separate. |
| Credited takedown | One newly confirmed rival wreck caused by player | Hard slam recipe plus a distinctive short metal/debris punctuation. Briefly duck engine/music about 4–6 dB for 0.35–0.7 s; during the existing slow-motion beat, lower the crash-tail playback rate smoothly toward the current `sqrt(timeScale)` presentation rate. Do not retrigger for subsequent contacts with the same wreck. |
| Player wreck | One newly confirmed player wreck | Full hard-hit body, conditional glass, 0.7–1.5 s settling metal/debris. Duck engine during the 1.5 s wreck/recovery presentation, then restore with a short fade; avoid replaying the full bang as the body bounces. |
| Distant rival-versus-rival | Newly confirmed AI hit/wreck away from player | Spatialized, quieter attack plus short metal tail. Attenuate and low-pass with distance; omit low thump and mix duck beyond about 80 m. An uncredited RIVAL DOWN gets no player takedown sting or slow-motion audio. |

**Variation and priorities.** Prepare at least three attack/body alternatives across light, medium and hard tiers; avoid the last two choices for the same tier. Randomize each selected clip's rate about ±4–6%, start offset by at most 20–30 ms where the attack remains intact, and vary the body/metal ratio with zone and severity. Never randomize so much that a light knock sounds heavier than a takedown. For a close hard hit, target at most three sample voices at once (attack, body/crunch, conditional glass or debris); bake some authoring layers into a composite where needed. Preserve the current five continuous voices and ten transient slots, prioritizing player and nearby events over distant AI ticks. Use existing per-pair cooldown/queue behavior; sustained grind is a stateful loop, not one impact per physics step. Keep roughly 6 dB of mix headroom, use a gentle limiter on the effects bus, and audition on headphones and small speakers for clipping and intelligibility.

**Browser delivery.** Reuse the game's activated Howler/Web Audio context; schedule sound starts and gain ramps on the audio clock after the physics step. [Browsers restrict autoplay](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices), and [`decodeAudioData`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/BaseAudioContext/decodeAudioData) decodes complete files into memory. Keep boot free of crash-bank requests until a real user gesture, then load a small core bank; lazy-load richer hard-hit variants after the driving view appears. Edit the 7–9 s recordings down to useful excerpts. Target **≤0.5 MB first audio activation**, **≤1.5 MB total added compressed crash audio on desktop**, **≤0.7 MB added on mobile**, and **≤6 MB decoded crash buffers on mobile**; measure actual production assets, not their source WAV sizes. Export mono 48 kHz compressed effects where spatial placement matters, test an alternate broadly supported encoding if a target browser cannot decode the chosen Ogg, and keep stereo only where its width materially improves a near, non-spatial crash. On mobile, reduce simultaneous debris/low-end voices before dropping the attack or scrape. Mute, pause and context suspension must stop/fade loops and discard stale queued hits as the current output does.

**Approval audition.** A/B the same natural drive with current audio and the first edited bank: slow rear knock, medium side hit, boosted hard slam, wall slide, long grind, player wreck, credited takedown and distant AI pileup. Check that light contact is audible, metal deformation lasts longer than the first transient, grind starts/stops with contact, glass is earned, and engine returns cleanly after slow motion. Measure compressed download, decoded memory, active voices and frame cost on the UHD 620 box and a mobile browser before calling the mix done.
